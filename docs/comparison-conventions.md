# Comparison conventions — Phase 3A

This is the contract for a future comparison of **geocentric ecliptic longitude** of Mars. Latitude is deferred until each historical model has a sourced latitude theory. A shared physical instant and a shared labelled coordinate system are required before a longitude residual has meaning. No modern ephemeris adapter or frame transformation is implemented here.

## Chronology and time

| Layer | Convention | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Civil label | Explicit proleptic Julian or Gregorian calendar, astronomical integer year (0 = 1 BCE, −1 = 2 BCE); BCE/CE is display only. A civil day fraction begins at midnight. | Integer calendar arithmetic is exact by the chosen *proleptic* rule; neither calendar claims historical civil use at every date. |
| Julian Day | JD changes integer at noon; integer JDN denotes that noon. `civil_to_local_jd` subtracts 0.5 for civil midnight. | Exact convention, subject to floating-point precision for fractional days. |
| Common instant | `ScaledJulianDay(value, "UT1")` is an assumed Earth-rotation instant. The `scale` tag prevents a TT JD being passed to a local-time conversion. UTC is not used as a synonym for ancient UT1. | Ancient UT1 reconstruction and the identity of a historical observation instant may be uncertain. |
| Historical local mean time | `LMT JD = UT1 JD + east longitude/360`. The caller supplies the meridian; no Alexandria longitude is embedded as an unsourced constant. | Conditional on the chosen historical meridian and the uniform mean-time convention. Historical apparent solar time would require an equation-of-time step. |
| Ptolemy | `t = Alexandria LMT JD − 1448638`; `t=0` is local noon on astronomical Julian −746-02-26, 1 Thoth Nabonassar year 1. Each Egyptian year has 365 days, with no leap day. | The epoch correlation is a scholarly calendar convention (P2 HAMA VI.A.2 p.1066; see [Mars specification](mars-almagest.md)); the original historical day-count interpretation is preserved. |
| Copernicus | Convert the *same* instant through a separately sourced meridian, calendar, epoch, day boundary, and mean-motion convention. | Not yet specified numerically; do not reuse Ptolemy's epoch or Alexandria meridian. |
| Modern reference | `TT JD = UT1 JD + ΔT/86400`, where ΔT = TT − UT1 and its source and uncertainty must be recorded. TDB requires a further documented conversion; no TDB routine is implemented. | Ancient ΔT is model dependent and uncertain. Vary ΔT over a stated range when reporting ancient residuals; never silently interpret historical LMT as TT/TDB. |

`ptolemy_days_at_ut1` is only a conditional bridge from an explicitly tagged UT1 JD and a supplied Alexandria longitude to the existing continuous Egyptian day argument. It does not change `evaluate_mars(days)` or the browser's date display. A future modern adapter must declare its ephemeris time scale, kernel coverage, ΔT model, and conversion chain. For an assumed instant, exact calendar and meridian arithmetic should be reported separately from historical uncertainty in ΔT, source dates, and longitude of the meridian.

## Coordinate and observable contract

The first observable is a **geocentric** ecliptic longitude increasing eastward, normalized to `[0°, 360°)`. Every sample must name the body, physical instant, observer, ecliptic/equinox frame identifier, and position kind. `LongitudeSample` and `longitude_residual` reject mismatched metadata; they perform no transformation. The residual is first minus second wrapped to `[-180°, 180°)`. Time-series display may separately unwrap successive samples for a continuous curve; unwrapped angles are not single-position residuals.

Ptolemy's implemented longitude is tropical relative to his equinoctial origin with his apsidal precession (P1 IX.5–6 pp.442–444; P8 ch.9 pp.262–263). Copernicus uses both sidereal/fixed-star and tropical references in different derivations (C1 III.1–12 and V.15–19; C3 pp.349–351); his output frame must be stated per calculation. A modern fixed frame such as an ICRF-oriented vector is not an ecliptic/equinox-of-date longitude. To compare either historical result with modern ecliptic-of-date longitude, specify a precession, ecliptic, and equinox mapping, with its authority and date argument. Do not merely give both values the label “longitude.”

Use **geometric** for instantaneous centre-to-centre directions before propagation and observer corrections. **Astrometric** includes light-time to the target and is expressed in a declared reference frame. **Apparent** further depends on the chosen aberration, gravitational deflection, precession/nutation, and observer convention; topocentric apparent position also involves parallax and the observing site. These terms must accompany, rather than be hidden in, a modern reference. Ptolemy's historically named “apparent” table longitude is a computed geocentric direction from his theory, not automatically the modern apparent-place observable. The current planar Mars evaluator has no latitude, physical distance, light-time, aberration, nutation, parallax, or refraction.

Residuals against ancient observations combine observational and recording uncertainty, uncertain dates and time standards, historical model error, numerical table/rounding error, and any modern reference/frame conversion uncertainty. A residual against modern prediction alone is not an observation-error estimate. Report the source date, frame mapping, position kind, ΔT assumption, and both calculation methods with each plotted or tabulated comparison.

## First model dependency: *De revolutionibus* Mars

The Copernican geocentric Mars direction needs **both** Copernicus's Earth annual motion and his Mars construction at the same instant. C1 III.13–25 gives the Earth/solar inequalities, motions, epoch, and tables; III.1–12 gives precession/equinox treatment and III.26 the equation of time. C1 V.1–4 gives planetary mean motions and general geometry; V.15–18 the Mars derivation, parameters, and epoch positions; V.19 the Earth/Mars scale relation; V.33–34 the correction tables and longitude procedure. C3 ch.3 pp.127–188 analyses Earth's motions and Copernican chronology/geography; ch.5 pp.289–323 gives the general longitude construction, pp.349–366 Mars and its textual/parameter variants, and pp.443–459 the tables and their use. C3 pp.350–351 specifically distinguishes the ancient opposition reconstruction (`e1=1500`, `r′=500` with radius `10000`) from the later observations (`e1=1460`, `r′=500`); these must not be mixed silently. These are research locators, **not** a complete verified parameter set for implementation.

The eventual record should be body specific and versioned: `(tradition, work, edition, variant, body, observable)` plus each sourced motion/submodel dependency, parameter notation and units, epoch, meridian, frame, and compatibility version. A Mars record should point to a **specific historical Earth-motion version** and reject an incompatible epoch or geometric assumption. It should declare only supported observables, initially geocentric longitude. Phase 3B should select one attested C1/C3 reading, transcribe its Earth annual motion and Mars constants with exact locators, resolve the sidereal-to-tropical mapping and epoch/time rule, then validate Earth and Mars separately against source examples before composing a geocentric longitude. No general dependency engine or substitute Earth orbit is warranted yet.
