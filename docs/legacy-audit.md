# Phase 0 audit: SOL and CALTIB snapshots

Audited local snapshots under `private/legacy/` read-only. This is a code and source survey, not a numerical validation. Public upstream repositories: [SOL](https://github.com/gantemur/sol) and [CALTIB](https://github.com/gantemur/caltib). Do not assume these local files match current upstream revisions.

## SOL planetary pages

`sol-planets-en/` and `sol-planets-mn/` are both present. The English index links Sun, Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, and Saturn; it includes two solar pages: `sun_alm.html` and `sun_alf.html`. `earth.html` is a separate Sun–Earth–Moon display, not another historical planetary theory. The English and Mongolian `almagest.js` and `palmagest.js` mathematical files compare identical; interface copy is separate in pages. Translation was therefore handled by duplication, not a central catalogue.

The interactive pages load `palmagest.js` (constants and date/angle helpers), `planets.js` (date input, keyboard, speed/trail sliders, resize, animation timer), and one drawing module: `sun.js`, `moon.js`, `sup.js` for Venus/Mars/Jupiter/Saturn, or `merc.js`. They draw deferent, epicycle, equant, mean Sun, projected geocentric longitude, and a position trail on a Canvas. The canvas click or Space key toggles playback; controls and text live in each HTML page. `mars.html` is a separate older self-contained example with decimal rates, not the source of the shared-page parameters.

`almagest.js` is a separate, large form-based ephemeris calculator. It holds sexagesimal constants, functions `eqplan` and `eqme`, calendar conversions, and calculated positions for all seven bodies. Its `latout` is called for Mars, Jupiter, and Saturn, and it computes lunar, Venus, and Mercury latitude paths too. **These are unused by the Canvas animation pages**, which import `palmagest.js` and use longitude-only `draw()` functions. Thus the latitude code is not dead globally; it is unintegrated with the animation architecture. The inherited outer-planet latitude formula and the more elaborate inner-planet formulae need independent historical and numerical checks before use.

`sun_alm.html` sets solar apsidal motion to zero; `sun_alf.html` sets `napogeesu = 1` degree per `cent = 36525` days while retaining the same solar constants. The index calls the latter Alfonsine-inspired and sidereally fixed. Other animation pages use `apogee0 + ddays/cent`, including pages named `_alm`. **Correction from Phase 1:** the moving *planetary* apogee is explicit in *Almagest* IX.5–6 (P1 pp.443–444), 1° per **100 Egyptian years**, so its presence in `_alm` is historically appropriate. The SOL denominator `36525` instead of historical `36500` days causes a small but real rate difference. The solar fixed/moving variants have separate historical status. `almagest.js` also offers a Theon-style sidereal longitude correction, a separate display convention. Do not generalize one body's apsidal rule to another.

Useful ideas to salvage after verification: visual vocabulary for Earth/deferent/equant/epicycle; intermediate geometric points; synchronized playback and trails; original sexagesimal arrays as leads for source checking; calculator examples as secondary regression candidates. Reimplement mathematics as pure functions and derive screen coordinates from their returned geometry. No legacy code was ported.

### Assumptions and likely defects to test

- The animation's `jdn2ndn` epoch offset and proleptic Gregorian `date2jdn` require documented epoch, noon/midnight, and BCE conventions. Integer date input loses time of day. Values labeled “longitude” do not declare ecliptic/equinox or apparent-place corrections.
- `cent = 36525` (with a commented `36500` alternative) differs from the 36500 Egyptian days implied by P1 IX.5–6 for the planetary 1°/century apsidal rate. The solar fixed/rotating versions remain distinct.
- Normalized deferent radius, independently scaled drawing lengths, hard-coded epochs in HTML, and modern pixel-radius choices have no guaranteed physical distance meaning. Treat the plotted “celestial sphere” as a visualization device.
- `almagest.js` uses many unscoped assignments and repeated calls to functions whose outputs depend on a numeric selector. It is coupled to DOM form fields. Trigonometric corrections often use `atan(numerator/denominator)` rather than quadrant-aware `atan2`; check domains and signs near exceptional configurations before reuse.
- `moon.js` assigns `tlong` twice; the earlier latitude-argument-derived value is overwritten before display. Its lunar phase graphic is schematic, not a validated photometric phase model. `sup.js` and `merc.js` still contain copy-pasted Mars comments. These are concrete signs that displayed behavior must be checked against calculations.
- Screen resizing and controls are global and page-specific. A mobile-first two-model comparison and centralized translation mechanism need new composition rather than a direct merge of the two language snapshots.

## Implementation lineage and Phase 1 Mars check

The local SOL English index (`sol-planets-en/index.html`, lines 52–59) explicitly says the implementation is partly based on [van Gent's calculator](https://webspace.science.uu.nl/~gent0113/astro/almagestephemeris_main.htm) and [Duke's animations](https://people.sc.fsu.edu/~dduke/models). This establishes an acknowledged dependency, but does not say which source line or numerical expression was copied. The separate sources and their access/reuse limits are recorded in [online-resources.md](online-resources.md).

| Layer | Evidence in the local snapshot | Interpretation and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Historical numbers | `palmagest.js` lines 54–58 and `almagest.js` lines 54–58 contain Mars sexagesimal daily rates, apogee `106;40`, epicycle `39;30`, eccentricity `6`; `mars_alm.html` supplies epoch phases `3;32` and `327;13`. | These equal P1 IX.3–4 and X.7–10 values. SOL and van Gent agreement on them is not a second source check. Their exact route into SOL cannot be separated from the acknowledgement alone. |
| Formula family | `palmagest.js`/`almagest.js` `eqplan` computes correction angles from normalized equant geometry; `sup.js` uses the same correction structure and draws explicit centres. | A formula adopted or adapted from a calculator is plausible; this audit has not established literal line-by-line copying. The Phase 1 Python formula was independently derived from the documented construction and separately checked against Cartesian positions. |
| Animation geometry | Duke's [published Mars notes](https://people.sc.fsu.edu/~dduke/technotes.htm) use Earth, deferent centre, equant, epicycle centre, planet, trail, and screen scaling. Local older `mars.html` lines 242–253 share his six-decimal rates `0.524051`, `0.461576` and radii `60`, `6`, `39.5`. | Strong evidence of inspiration or adaptation for the older display, not a verified code-copy claim. Duke's `t=0` is an arbitrary animation origin; no historical civil epoch is documented there. The shared `mars_alm.html` instead uses sexagesimal rates and historical epoch phases. |
| SOL-specific choices | `mars_alm.html`/`sup.js` use Gregorian date widgets and `jdn2ndn`, 36525-day apogee century, rescaled Canvas coordinates, localization in duplicated pages, and a particular screen/trail/UI scheme. `almagest.js` adds form outputs including latitude, physical-distance, parallax, and a sidereal option. | The date-to-Alexandria offset and historical noon convention need explicit matching before numerical comparisons; display coordinates have no historical unit meaning. A modernizing UI choice is not a Ptolemaic parameter. |

For a same-`t` diagnostic, the shared-page SOL `eqplan` formula was evaluated locally with its own Mars constants, and the Phase 1 Cartesian evaluator gave identical epoch longitude `357.484854684°`. At Nabonassar 450 X 8 (`t=164162`), SOL yielded `47.660398860°` and Phase 1 yielded `47.660458275°`; the ~`0.0000594°` gap follows chiefly from `36525` versus `36500` for apsidal motion. These two implementations share constants and equation structure, so this is **not independent historical validation**. The older `mars.html` uses Duke-like rounded rates: its mean-longitude rate is `0.524051°/day` versus P1's `0.524059711355…°/day`; it is not a source-verified ephemeris with the shared page's epoch. van Gent's interactive output and Duke's Flash output were not run in Phase 1. No third-party code or licensing was altered.

## CALTIB reference and research code

`private/legacy/caltib/` is a Python calendar research project, not an Epicycle Atlas dependency decision. `pyproject.toml` declares Python ≥3.9 with optional numerical/ephemeris extras. Its reference layer has good reusable design patterns, but the historical models and modern references must remain separate.

| Component | Advantages and possible use | Limitations / checks before reuse |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `reference/build_micro_vsop.py`, `planets_tmpl.py`, generated `planets.py` | Generator downloads full VSOP87D source at build time, keeps `L/B/R` terms satisfying `abs(A) * 3^power >= threshold` (default `5e-5`), writes self-contained Python. This suggests generation of compact browser data with no runtime API. | Source URLs, dataset revision/checksums, parser assumptions, licensing, and generated coefficients must be pinned. `tau = (JD_TT − J2000)/365250`; the factor 3 is only a term-selection heuristic over roughly ±3000 years, not an error bound. Earth's generated latitude series is empty at this threshold. |
| `reference/planets.py` | Six heliocentric planets and geocentric Sun/planets; simple spherical/vector conversion and EMB-to-Earth lunar offset. | Geocentric output uses a one-term Moon by default for EMB correction; it is geometric and lacks a declared apparent-place correction. Generated data and errors across historical epochs must be assessed. |
| `reference/lunar.py`, `solar.py` | Compact series for Moon and Sun, with ecliptic positions and distances. | Truncated analytical approximations; source comments make accuracy claims that are not a substitute for independent epoch-wide residuals. Keep separate from historical Moon/Sun models. |
| `reference/kepler.py` | A still lighter secular Keplerian fallback and coordinate conversion. | Modern orbital elements are approximations and cannot represent historical parameters or high-precision reference by themselves. Validate its date span before use. |
| `reference/astro_args.py`, `coords.py` | Fundamental arguments, mean obliquity, J2000-to-ecliptic-of-date matrix, sidereal/horizontal/topocentric transformations. | State whether precession/nutation and observer effects are included; some labels say “apparent” while helper formulae use mean sidereal time or geometric positions. Verify frame conventions and epoch validity. |
| `reference/time_scales.py`, `deltat.py`, `core/time.py` | JD/JDN helpers, UTC/TT conversion, monthly modern ΔT table plus polynomial fallback, rational calendar arithmetic. | `datetime.date`/`datetime` paths cannot represent BCE years. UTC/UT1 default offsets and ancient polynomial ΔT uncertainty must be explicit. Add integer-year proleptic Julian/Gregorian conversion for Atlas rather than silently applying Gregorian dates. |
| `reference/events.py`, `stars.py`, `build_stars.py` | Event root finding and compact stellar reference machinery; may support later diagnostics. | Event functions inherit reference/time-scale assumptions; stellar data and generated provenance require separate audit before use. Not needed for first Mars model. |
| `ephemeris/de422.py` | Loads local DE422 data via `jplephem.Ephemeris` and computes geocentric Moon–Sun elongation from vectors. Runtime evaluations do not call an online ephemeris service. | Facade is specialized to elongation/lunar events; a general vector adapter is cleaner for planetary comparisons. Optional `de422`, `jplephem`, and NumPy are needed, with external kernel/data installation. A fixed J2000 obliquity rotation here is not an ecliptic-of-date transform. Verify actual dataset coverage rather than assuming it from a filename. |
| `diagnostics/ephem/validate_ref_planets.py` | Existing pattern compares sampled geocentric longitude/latitude/distance with local DE422 and writes plots; its configured JD clipping spans about astronomical years −3000 to +3000. | It reports plots, not numeric maxima/percentiles or reproducible fixture tables; has duplicate dictionary initialization. Compare like with like: its vectors are geometric and transformed to ecliptic of date, not observed apparent positions. The hard-coded bounds are not a substitute for inspecting installed kernel metadata. |
| `engines/`, `design/`, tests | Traditional Tibetan calendar and rational/float astronomical arithmetic; examples of separated engines, diagnostics, mixed-radix calculations and test organization. | Domain-specific calendar engines should not be adopted as historical planetary models. `test_astro_args*` and `test_time_scales*` exercise present-era/J2000 examples but do not establish BCE calendar behavior or planetary-reference accuracy. |

Generation-time network access occurs in `build_micro_vsop.py`; the generated `planets.py` evaluates offline. The DE422 adapter also evaluates offline once local ephemeris data is installed. No generator, DE422 validation, or end-to-end numerical accuracy test was run in Phase 0, so no precision claim is made.
