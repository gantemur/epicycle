# Planetary Map — spatial and angular contracts

The Phase 6A **Spatial Map** below remains the Copernican common-scale construction. Phase 6B adds an **Angular Map** representation to the same workspace display. Switching modes retains their separate settings; their circles and distances have different meanings.

## Spatial Map (Phase 6A)

The Map is a display of the existing 1543 Copernican Earth, Mars, Jupiter, and Saturn constructions, not a fifth historical model. It shows the true Sun, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, and Saturn in the native `copernicus-1543-sidereal` ecliptic plane. It shows no latitude or construction circles. Its Sun marker is S, the true Sun; G, the historical mean Sun, is an evaluator intermediate and not a map body.

One assumed UT1 Julian Day is converted once by `modelDaysAtUt1(ut1Jd).copernicus` to Copernican Frauenburg local days. All four evaluators use that day. Earth and true Sun come from the Earth record. Each planet's `toCommonEarthUnits()` conversion reverses the planet-specific Earth scaling, using its versioned record: Mars 6580, Jupiter 1916, Saturn 1090 original planetary units for Earth's annual radius. The common length unit has Earth annual-circle radius 10000. It is a historical geometric unit, not a modern astronomical unit. C1 III.13–25 and V.1–19, C2 1543 folios, and the source audits in this directory establish the underlying constructions; this display introduces no new fit.

For body position X_i and chosen centre c, the map plots Y_i(t) = X_i(t) − X_c(t). The centre is always (0,0); recentering never rotates the sidereal axes. A relative direction is `atan2(Y_i.y,Y_i.x)` wrapped to 0°–360°, and distance is `hypot(Y_i.x,Y_i.y)` in common units. Earth-centred directions are geometric sidereal longitudes. Other-centred directions are relative angles, not geocentric observations. There is no light-time, topocentric, or apparent-place correction and no modern frame calibration.

The Map requires an explicit Mars apsis policy. `sn-local-uniform-1473-1523` uses the existing inclusive software guard; `sn-uniform-exploratory` continues the same scholarly uniform estimate outside it. The Map preset deliberately selects exploratory continuation, visibly labelled. If guarded Mars is unavailable, the other bodies remain available. If Mars is the selected centre, no relative position is drawn until Mars is available or the user changes the centre/policy. Jupiter and Saturn's broad-date uniform mean motions are mathematical continuations, not demonstrated observational accuracy.

Each past trail evaluates **both** the body and its selected centre at every sampled instant: Trail_i(t) = X_i(t) − X_c(t). The selected centre has no orbital trail. Unavailable samples are omitted and segments break at provenance or availability boundaries. Exact positions at the requested start and live end are joined to cached historical-day grid samples; the Mars guard endpoints are sampled exactly. The cache key includes system, policy, centre, duration, cadence, sampled window and selected trails. A 30-year trail uses a six-day cadence (about 61 samples per Julian year, at most 1,826 ordinary samples); shorter trails use a three-day cadence. The long SVG path changes only when its grid window or configuration changes, while endpoint and marker positions follow the live clock. This resolution is a visual approximation of the continuous historical geometry, not a different evaluator.

The Map offers two presentation projections. True scale plots the relative historical vector Y directly. The preset selects radial compression: for r=|Y| and fixed a=10000 original units, f(r)=a asinh(r/a) and W(Y)=f(r)Y/r, with W(0)=0. Historical positions are first translated to the selected centre, then projected, then translated and scaled by the panel camera. The warp is radial around the **historical** centre even after camera pan. It preserves angular direction and radial order but distorts visible distances, path shapes and screen-space speeds. All numerical distances and angles remain those of Y, not W(Y); compression is not a new Copernican prediction. The bilingual notice remains visible when settings are closed.

Camera offsets and radius are in projected display coordinates. Switching modes maps the camera target and radius through the inverse/forward radial functions, so the same historical target remains in view. Fit considers projected visible markers and enabled trail samples, including their ends, with margins. Pan, zoom and reset are deliberate panel operations; ordinary animation never refits. Guide rings are drawn at original distances 10000, 30000, 60000 and 100000 using the selected projection; a bilingual legend lists those original distances from centre outward without placing labels over moving bodies. The historical trail cache is independent of projection, while its SVG path is rebuilt when projection changes. The workspace stores validated serializable Map settings under its versioned schema and restores playback paused. This phase does not add browser persistence.

The generated Python fixture checks 1512 and 2026 positions and pairwise distances against the original Python Earth, Mars, Jupiter and Saturn evaluators. Frontend tests compare those positions and common-scale factors with TypeScript, check pairwise-vector invariance under all viewing centres, match Earth-centred sidereal directions to the existing longitude evaluators, and check moving-centre trails and policy gaps. The historical source cases remain separate from this generated display fixture.

On an Apple M3 Pro MacBook Pro (12 cores, 36 GB), Node 26.4.0, five-run medians for four trails were 0.36 ms for 90 days (31 grid samples), 0.89 ms for 365 days (123), 2.88 ms for five years (610), and 8.40 ms for 30 years (1,826); corresponding SVG-string generation was 0.05, 0.10, 0.39 and 1.33 ms. Two 30-year Maps with different centres took 16.91 ms combined to sample. These are pure computation timings, not browser frame timings. An interactive four-panel browser check (two Maps, geometry and longitude plot) showed responsive controls and continuous playback; trail grids remained cached between grid-boundary changes. Browser timings depend on layout and device.

For the radial-projection refinement, a nine-run median on the same macOS/Node 26.4.0 environment with four 30-year trails was 12.17 ms for historical sampling, 1.60 ms for linear SVG generation, and 1.98 ms for compressed SVG generation. The measured presentation cost was about 0.38 ms for those four paths. Switching projection retains the historical sample cache; these timings exclude browser layout and painting.

## Angular Map (Phase 6B deployed)

Earth is fixed at the centre. Each track selects an implemented geocentric **tropical** longitude evaluator independently through `traceLongitudeAt()`. For body b, model m and shared UT1 instant t, the display point is `ρ_b(cos λ_m,b(t), sin λ_m,b(t))`. The longitude never depends on `ρ_b`. The four fixed illustrative ring radii are 25%, 45%, 65% and 85% of the drawing radius, outward in the Sun–Mars–Jupiter–Saturn order Ptolemy adopts in *Almagest* IX.1. *Planetary Hypotheses* I.16–17 supplies conditional physical-sphere bounds, recorded separately in [the source audit](historical-planetary-distances.md); those bounds are **not** used as ring radii or instantaneous positions. Copernican points use the same illustrative Ptolemaic-order layout and do not inherit Ptolemaic physical distances.

The historical-system selector fills the four currently supported bodies with Ptolemaic or Copernican models; Custom allows individual selection and at most two models per body, eight tracks total. Two predictions for one body share the exact same ring. A filled and an outlined marker distinguish them without displacing their angles. Readouts give each longitude and the signed second-minus-first principal difference for a same-body pair. This is model disagreement, not observational error. The Map offers no angular Sun-centred observer or physical distance scale.

Native frame mode accepts only mutually compatible visible historical tropical frames. Mixed Ptolemy–Copernicus tracks require the explicitly chosen **nominal historical tropical alignment**, a zero-offset identification of intended directions, not calibration to a modern frame. Cross-body angles are not labelled model disagreement. The original model ID, per-track date policy and source availability are retained. Source-limited Copernican Mars disappears outside the selected 1473–1523 software guard while other tracks remain; exploratory continuation requires an explicit policy selection and is dashed outside that interval.

Past trails sample each existing longitude at historical times on an aligned three-day grid, or six-day grid for 30 years. Each path stays on its assigned circular track, following direct and retrograde motion; null samples break gaps. The exact live marker and trail endpoint use the actual clock instant. Sampled historical longitudes are cached by body, model, policy, frame convention, duration, cadence and window; illustrative styling does not resample them. No new evaluator or historical parameters are introduced. The version 3 workspace schema keeps separate spatial and angular subconfigurations, migrates valid version 1 and 2 states, and restores playback paused. No browser storage is added.

Future spatial extensions require compatible historical distance records for every included body. The Angular representation can also host source-backed longitude-only models, after their time coverage and continuity are established. Neither representation supplies latitude or a physical observing location.
